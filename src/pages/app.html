The Cherry App: How the Platform Works for Hosts and Agencies

The Cherry recruiting materials describe a video-chat app. Users pay for calls and send gifts, hosts earn diamonds for that activity, and agencies earn a weekly commission on the earnings of the hosts they bring in. This page explains how those pieces fit together.

Source: supplied Cherry recruiting policy. Content reviewed {{REVIEWED}}.

What the Cherry app is

According to its recruiting policy, the Cherry app is a platform built around video conversation. The policy describes several kinds of paid interaction:

  • Video calls, which pay hosts per minute
  • Match calls, which also pay per minute, at a lower rate
  • Gifts, which users send to hosts
  • Text chat, listed among the earnings that count towards agency commission
  • Live sessions, which hosts can be restricted from after repeated warnings

This website is not the app's publisher. We describe the platform only as far as the documents supplied to us describe it. We do not have access to the app's internal systems, user numbers or ratings, and we don't quote any.

How money moves, in brief

  1. Users spend on calls, gifts and chat.
  2. Hosts earn diamonds from that activity.
  3. Diamonds are valued at 10,000 = $1.
  4. Agencies earn commission on their hosts' weekly earnings.
  5. Hosts and agencies request withdrawals, which are reviewed before payment.

How the Cherry earning system is described

Host earnings on the Cherry app are counted in diamonds, not cash. The stated value is 10,000 diamonds = $1, so a balance of 1,000,000 diamonds is worth $100.

Hosts can withdraw once they hold at least 100,000 diamonds ($10). The Cherry earnings page has a full conversion table and a converter.
